What we do

Support when care becomes complex

SAPV complements care provided by family doctors, nursing and hospice services when symptoms are severe or change rapidly.

01

24-hour availability

We are available around the clock, including weekends.

02

Relieving symptoms

For example pain, breathlessness, anxiety, restlessness, nausea or vomiting.

03

Advice and support

We support patients and relatives with decisions and during crises.

04

Coordinating care

We coordinate medicines, medical aids and participating services.

05

Staying at home

We help to avoid burdensome hospital admissions whenever possible.

06

Connecting partners

We work with family doctors, nursing services, hospice services and other specialists.

How we work together

A person remains a person.

The patient is at the centre of our care. We listen, explain clearly and respect personal decisions, even when a recommendation is not accepted immediately.

Serious situations require seriousness, but not every minute has to feel heavy. A kind word, a light-hearted moment or gentle banter can have a place when it feels right for everyone involved.

When SAPV may help

Typical severe symptoms

These examples provide a simple overview. The medical assessment, legal requirements and the health insurer’s decision are decisive.

Severe pain

Pain remains difficult to control despite treatment or changes significantly.

Neurological, psychological or emotional distress

For example confusion, anxiety, agitation, seizures or severe emotional distress.

Breathing or cardiovascular symptoms

For example breathlessness, fear of suffocation, chest tightness or severe circulatory problems.

Gastrointestinal symptoms

For example nausea, vomiting, bowel obstruction, swallowing problems or distressing abdominal fluid pressure.

Complex wounds or ulcerating tumours

For example open, odorous, bleeding or difficult-to-treat wounds.

Urogenital symptoms

For example urinary problems, bleeding, fistulas or severe kidney problems.

Other severe symptoms

For example severe itching, metabolic imbalance or persistent distressing hiccups.

i Admission is governed by statutory rules and the SAPV directive. A medical prescription is required. The health insurer decides whether the service is approved and may seek advice from the Medical Service, known as MD and formerly MDK. For people with statutory health insurance, approved SAPV services are paid for by the health insurer.

Why SAPV Friedland?

Many people wish to remain in familiar surroundings for as long as possible. Our team helps patients and families gain confidence and reduce burdens. Together, we look for solutions that suit the individual situation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who can receive SAPV?

People with an incurable, progressive illness, limited life expectancy and particularly complex care needs may be eligible. Each case is assessed individually.

Do I need a hospital admission?

No. SAPV is outpatient care designed to support care in familiar surroundings.

Do I have to change my family doctor?

No. SAPV complements existing care and coordinates with the professionals already involved.

Are there any costs?

For people with statutory health insurance, approved SAPV services are paid for by the health insurer.

Can I call at weekends?

Yes. Our 24-hour number is also available at weekends.

Can I choose another SAPV team?

Your right to choose matters to us. Please clarify the specific service and area with your health insurer and chosen team.

How does the on-call service work?

Call the central number. Calls to a nurse’s personal work phone are automatically forwarded to the nurse on duty outside working hours.

Work with SAPV Friedland

Work independently. Carry responsibility together.

We value professional expertise, autonomous work, short decision paths and respectful cooperation. Training and teamwork are part of good palliative care.
